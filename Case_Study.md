# **&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; 2. Historical Devolopment :**

E_ commerce, which is short for electronic commerce,is the buying but also selling of goods and services online,mainly through what we call nowadays the internet. in the modern day, shopping online is something most people do without giving a second thought. ecommerce has significantly developed over numerous decades, starting with simple computer systems and eventually growing into the online website we know today.Amazon and ebay both played a massive part in changing the way people shop today.

EARLY FOUNDATIONS (EDI)
in the years 1960 through 70, Businesses began using computers to share and process commercial information, one key development was electronic EDI this allowed companies to transmit invoices and purchases orders electronically instead of relying solely on the papers(UNIVERSITY OF LONDON N.D) This made communication between businesses and other companies quicker and helped reduce paperwork and delays. although technology was expensive and unaffordable and mainly used by larger companies,it was an unimportant starting point for what would eventually become mordern e commerce(university of london n.d)
The Introduction of Electronic Shopping (1979)

The Introduction of Electronic Shopping (1979)
A massive leap towards online shopping came through in 1979 when British inventor Michael Aldrich began developing an idea known as teleshopping (Aldrich Archive, n.d.). He connected a modified television to a computer through a telephone line, allowing customers to view shopping information and place orders from the comfort of their own house . At the time, this was quite a new idea because people no longer had to visit a shop in person to make a purchase. Even though technology was unaffordable  and never became widely available until a little while later , Aldrich helped introduce the idea that shopping could be done remotely, something that has become completely normal today (Aldrich Archive, n.d.).

The Growth of Internet Shopping (1990s)
The early 1980s was a turning point for ecommerce because the internet started becoming more accessible to the public. With the introduction of websites and safer online payment technology, businesses had new opportunities to reach customers without needing a physical shop.
A person whole played a crucial role in this change was a man called jeff bezos  who founded Amazon in 1994. The first website amazon opened was in July 1995 as an online bookstore (History.com, 2025). Books were an amazing starting point because there were so many different titles available, making it difficult for traditional bookshops to stock everything. Selling online meant Amazon could offer customers a much wider selection and allow them to shop from home (History.com, 2025).

Coming in around the same time a man named Pierre Omidyar created Auction Web in September 1995, which later became known as eBay (eBay Inc., n.d.). Unlike Amazon, which originally sold products directly to customers, eBay allowed people to buy and sell items with each other through online auctions. Interestingly, its first sale was a broken laser pointer. This showed that the internet could bring together people looking to buy and sell all kinds of items, including second-hand and unusual products (eBay Inc., n.d.).

The Expansion of Amazon and eBay (1997–2000s)

As online shopping became more popular, both companies continued to grow and introduce new features. Amazon became a publicly traded company in 1997 and expanded its product range beyond books to include music and videos in 1999. In 2000, it also allowed other businesses to sell products through its website, helping turn Amazon into a much larger online marketplace (History.com, 2025).

eBay also experienced major growth during this period. It officially changed its name from Auction Web to eBay in 1997 and introduced a feedback system that allowed buyers and sellers to rate each other after transactions (eBay Inc., n.d.). This was particularly important because people were buying from strangers online and needed a reason to trust them. The feedback system helped customers feel more confident when making purchases. eBay also went public in 1998, allowing the company to continue expanding (eBay Inc., n.d.).

Modern E-Commerce (2000s–Present)

During the 2000s, online shopping became even more popular as internet connections improved and people became more comfortable making payments online. Amazon introduced its Prime membership in 2005, offering customers faster delivery through a subscription service. In 2006, it also launched Amazon Web Services (AWS), expanding into cloud computing (History.com, 2025).
The arrival of smartphones and shopping apps made online shopping even easier. People could now browse products, compare prices and place orders wherever they were, without needing a computer. Both Amazon and eBay adapted to these changes and became major names in online retail. Overall, e-commerce has come a long way from the early days of electronic business documents and teleshopping. The development of the internet, along with companies such as Amazon and eBay, has completely changed the shopping experience.

# **&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; 6. Conclusion :**

The rise of e-commerce, seen through the evolution of eBay and Amazon, is a small-scale picture of how digital commerce has evolved as a whole. From the Business Viewpoint, we see bold strategic innovation. Platform models, logistics networks, and data-driven personalisation created lasting competitive advantage and huge market value. From the Societal Viewpoint, we see two sides. Online shopping opened up access to goods and gave small sellers a global marketplace. It also created worrying dependencies that affect local businesses, workers, privacy, and equity.
This case teaches three key lessons for enterprise computing:

1. Business success and social benefit don't automatically line up, so a conscious effort is needed to balance them.

2. Moving commerce online changes more than technology. It reshapes how communities, jobs, and consumer habits are structured.

3. Ethics has to be built in from the start, not added later. This covers fair labour practices, data protection, and fair competition.

As future computing professionals, we must learn from this evolution and build solutions that are profitable and efficient, but also fair and sustainable. The eBay and Amazon story reminds us that with great computational power comes great social responsibility.
The significance goes beyond the strategies of two companies. The case shows how enterprise computing shapes modern life, which is why every development decision needs to weigh multiple viewpoints and their ethical implications.
